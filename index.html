Tidewater

Payment streams

Money that moves by the second.

A retainer that drains as the month passes instead of being invoiced in arrears. Fund it once and it accrues continuously; stop it and both sides take exactly what the clock already gave them. Nothing runs on a timer — the figure is derived from elapsed time, so a five-year stream costs the same to run as a five-minute one.

Blindside

Sealed-bid auctions

Nobody sees your bid until everyone's is in.

On an ordinary chain every bid is public the moment it's sent, so the last bidder wins by a penny without ever valuing the item. Here you post a hash and prove what it said only after bidding closes — and because you can deposit more than you bid, the amount you lock gives nothing away either.

Bellwether

Prediction markets

Ask a question. Let the money answer.

Both sides stake into one pot and whoever's right splits it — no bookmaker, no order book, and no way for the contract to owe more than it holds. The hard part isn't the money, it's who declares the fact, so the resolver is named before anyone stakes a dollar.

Why these are cheap enough to bother with

All three run on Arc, where USDC is the network's own gas token. A payer needs nothing but the dollars they're already sending — no second token, no bridge, no explaining what a wallet is before they can hand you twenty bucks. Settlement is final in under a second and the fee is a fraction of a cent, which is what makes splitting an 850-dollar gig four ways worth doing onchain instead of on a spreadsheet.

What none of them do

Nothing is delivered. These settle money, not goods. No contract here knows whether the item shipped or the work got done.

There are no admin keys. Nobody — including whoever deployed them — can redirect a payment, cancel someone's stream, or pick an auction winner. That also means nobody can undo a mistake for you.

They're unaudited, and this is a testnet. Every dollar shown is play money from a faucet. Each contract's core math is verified against a reproducible simulation — split conservation, stream solvency, auction settlement — and each of those is in the repo. That isn't the same as a professional review.
